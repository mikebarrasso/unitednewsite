<p><strong>Business owners often manage personal and business finances that affect each other: cash flow, taxes, retirement savings, health coverage, and succession. This guide explains how to coordinate them, compares common self-employed retirement plans, and offers an annual checklist. Options vary by entity and income, and each involves trade-offs.</strong></p>

<h2>Why Business and Personal Finances Need to Be Planned Together</h2>

<p>For most owners, the business is both an income source and a large share of net worth. A decision in one place tends to reach the other. Owner compensation changes the tax return. A retirement plan choice can affect what you owe for employees. A future sale shapes how much you need to save outside the business.</p>

<p>At the same time, keeping clear boundaries between personal and business accounts matters for record keeping and for liability protection. Planning together does not mean mixing the two. It means reviewing them side by side, ideally with your CPA and planner working from the same information. Our <a href="/who-we-serve/business-owners">business owners page</a> describes how our team approaches this.</p>

<h2>Managing Irregular Income</h2>

<p>Income that rises and falls makes budgeting harder. Practices that many owners find useful include:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Paying yourself a regular amount.</strong> Transfer a set draw or salary from the business account to your personal account on a schedule, and adjust it periodically.</li>
<li><strong>Holding a reserve.</strong> Households with variable income often keep a larger cash reserve than those with steady paychecks. The right size depends on how volatile your revenue is and how quickly you could reduce expenses.</li>
<li><strong>Saving in strong periods.</strong> Setting aside part of higher-income months can smooth the slower ones, though it requires discipline.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Taxes: Estimated Payments, Self-Employment Tax, and Entity Choice</h2>

<p>Without an employer withholding taxes from a paycheck, owners are generally responsible for paying as they go.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Estimated payments.</strong> Self-employed individuals generally pay income tax and self-employment tax through quarterly estimated payments. The IRS explains the process on its <a href="https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes">estimated taxes page</a>. How much to set aside depends on your income, deductions, and state, so a fixed percentage rule of thumb may miss the mark.</li>
<li><strong>Self-employment tax.</strong> The rate is 15.3%, made up of 12.4% for Social Security and 2.9% for Medicare, generally applied to 92.35% of net earnings, with the Social Security portion subject to an annual wage limit. See the <a href="https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc554">IRS overview</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Entity choice.</strong> A sole proprietorship, LLC, S corporation, and C corporation are taxed differently. An S corporation, for example, can change how income is split between salary and distributions, but owners must pay themselves reasonable compensation, and the choice adds administrative requirements. Whether a change helps depends on your numbers, so review it with your CPA before acting.</li>
<li><strong>Timing.</strong> The timing of income and expenses, depreciation, and charitable giving can affect a given year's taxes. These decisions have trade-offs and are best considered before year-end. See our <a href="/services/tax-planning">tax planning</a> and <a href="/services/tax-preparation">tax preparation</a> services.</li>
</ul>

<p>State-level tax rules and any elections available to pass-through entities are also worth reviewing with your CPA each year.</p>

<h2>Health Coverage</h2>

<p>Owners without group coverage often buy through the New York State of Health marketplace or through the business. Some considerations:</p>

<ul>
<li>Premium credits on the marketplace depend on household income, so income and retirement contributions can interact with the cost of coverage.</li>
<li>A high-deductible health plan can make you eligible for a Health Savings Account, which offers tax advantages if you meet the requirements. It also means a higher out-of-pocket exposure before coverage pays.</li>
<li>Self-employed health insurance premiums may be deductible in some situations. Confirm eligibility with your CPA.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Business Continuity and Succession</h2>

<p>Succession planning starts earlier than most owners expect, because the options depend on the years available to prepare. Common paths include a transfer to family, a sale to a partner or key employee, a sale to an outside buyer, or a planned wind-down. Each has different tax, valuation, and timing considerations.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Build a business that can run without you.</strong> Documented processes and a second layer of leadership can widen your options.</li>
<li><strong>Coordinate with your attorney.</strong> Buy-sell agreements, entity documents, and estate plans are drafted by an attorney. A financial plan can confirm they match your goals and cash-flow needs.</li>
<li><strong>Plan personal finances ahead of a sale.</strong> The tax result of a sale and the income you will need afterward are worth modeling in advance.</li>
</ul>

<h2>An Annual Business Owner Checklist</h2>

<ul>
<li>Review this year's estimated payments against actual income</li>
<li>Meet with your CPA before year-end about timing of income and expenses</li>
<li>Confirm the retirement plan still fits your staffing and profit level</li>
<li>Check your cash reserve against current revenue volatility</li>
<li>Review health coverage and any HSA eligibility for next year</li>
<li>Update beneficiary designations and entity, buy-sell, and estate documents with your attorney</li>
<li>Revisit your succession or exit timeline and what it means for personal savings</li>
</ul>

<h2>Key Takeaway</h2>

<p>The most useful planning for owners usually connects cash flow, taxes, retirement savings, and succession instead of handling each on its own. Reviewing them together each year can help each decision support the others.</p>
